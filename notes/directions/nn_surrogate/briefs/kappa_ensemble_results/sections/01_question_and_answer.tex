\begin{center}
{\large\bfseries The Vertical-Mixing Perturbation Ensemble: Results\\[2pt]
 and the Case for \kv{} as a Surrogate Input}\\[3pt]
{\small Tanvir Shahriar \quad$\cdot$\quad DINO configuration, MITgcm
 \quad$\cdot$\quad \today}
\end{center}

\hd{What was run, in one paragraph.}
An adjoint run answers, in a single backward computation, how sensitive a
chosen quantity is to everything the model depends on --- here, how the
basin's overturning transport at 26\dg N (the cost $J$) responds to changes in
temperature, salinity, surface forcing, and mixing, everywhere and at every
earlier time. The proposed ensemble tested whether those sensitivity maps
survive a change in the model's vertical diffusivity \kv, the parameter that
controls how quickly heat and salt mix vertically. Seven members scaled the
reference value ($1.2\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$) by factors of
$0.25$ to $32$; each ran ten years forward so the ocean could adjust to its
new mixing, then a five-year adjoint over the same window as an unperturbed
reference run. All nine runs completed. The reference reproduced the earlier
production adjoint bit-for-bit, so previous analysis of it carries over
unchanged.

\hd{The headline.}
\textbf{The sensitivity maps depend strongly on \kv{} --- in two distinct
ways --- and \kv{} should therefore be an input to the neural-network
surrogate.} First, the physics: even a modest halving or doubling of \kv{}
leaves the spatial pattern of the sensitivities recognisable but shifts their
overall amplitude by tens of percent, and the sensitivity \emph{to \kv{}
itself} changes shape and even sign across the ensemble. Second, the
numerics: at four of the seven settings the backward (adjoint) computation
becomes unstable and its output ends as noise, which limits how training data
can be generated at strongly perturbed mixing. Both facts argue the same way:
a surrogate trained at one mixing setting, or one that treats mixing as a
fixed constant, would not generalise across the range of \kv{} that ocean
models plausibly occupy.

\hd{The ensemble at a glance.}
``Pattern correlation'' measures whether a member's accumulated
temperature-sensitivity map has the same spatial structure as the
reference's (1 = identical structure, 0 = unrelated); ``departure lead''
is how far back in time the adjoint computation stays trustworthy before
instability takes over (out of the full 5 years).
